# The second version

This document describes the second version of the app: where it is served, what its views show, and where their code lives. The first version is described in [v1.md](v1.md). The two versions share the color scheme setting.

## Addresses

The second version is served under `/v2`. The Historical glottometry view is at `/v2` itself, and the Pollination guilds view at `/v2/pollination`.

## Historical glottometry

The view draws a glottometric diagram as a living cell. Historical Glottometry, a method of Alexandre François and Siva Kalyan, measures every candidate subgroup of a language family by the innovations its languages share, instead of forcing the family into a tree. A glottometric diagram draws each subgroup as a contour around its languages, nested and overlapping where languages belong to several subgroups.

The view offers a choice of families, each with its citation shown below the scene. It opens on the early Germanic languages:

- six early Germanic languages (Gothic, Old Norse, Old English, Old Frisian, Old Saxon, Old High German), with the 167 shared innovations of Agee's 2018 thesis, read from its Table 1. Languages are labelled by their usual abbreviations in the scene;
- the sample "Demo" family distributed with the Historical Glottometry engine: eighteen fictitious languages, named ⓁA to ⓁR, with 474 shared innovations and the coordinates of each language;
- the six Boni dialects of the Kenyan coast, with the 25 shared innovations of Elias (2019), read from Table 22 of that paper. The paper gives no coordinates.

- **Languages** are bright dots, each labelled at a corner chosen so that labels of nearby languages do not overlap. A family with coordinates is placed by them, with north upward and east to the right, and two arrows at the southwestern corner show those directions, each spanning half a degree. A family without coordinates is placed by likeness, so that languages sharing many innovations lie close together.
- **Subgroups** are translucent lens-shaped membranes enclosing their members. Every set of languages that shares at least one innovation, other than single languages and the whole family, is a candidate subgroup.
- A membrane's **wall is brighter and thicker** the higher the subgroup's subgroupiness.
- Membranes **shade** from one color for the smallest subgroups to another for the largest, and the whole family is one more membrane around everything.
- The whole body **undulates** slowly, so that the diagram reads as a cell with nested organelles rather than a static map.
- **Pointing** at a membrane shows a tooltip naming its subgroup, with its support, cohesiveness and subgroupiness; pointing at a language's dot names the language.

### Scores

For a candidate subgroup, following Kalyan and François (2018):

- **Support** is the number of innovations shared by exactly its members.
- **Cohesiveness** is the share of innovations involving any of its members that do not cut across it. An innovation cuts across a subgroup when it is shared by some but not all of the members together with languages outside it.
- **Subgroupiness** is support multiplied by cohesiveness.

### Controls

- The **Family** menu chooses which family is drawn.
- The **subgroupiness display threshold** slider hides the subgroups whose subgroupiness is below it, as in the online analyzer. It starts at 1.
- The collapsible **Appearance** panel adjusts the shape of the membranes (depth, margins, corner sharpness), their material (body opacity, edge strength and width, and a glassy, satin or matte finish), their colors, and the movement (amplitude, speed, and whether the camera turns on its own). A Reset button restores the defaults.
- The scene can be turned and zoomed with the pointer.
- The **What you are looking at** panel below the scene explains these elements and cites the family's data source and the paper the scores follow.

## Data

Each family's tables live under `src/client/v2/glottometry/` and are parsed in the browser; the view reads nothing from the Worker or the database. `families.ts` registers each family with its name, description and citation.

- `demo/`: the Demo family's two tables, copied verbatim from the engine's sample data (https://marama.huma-num.fr/AF_Glottometry.htm).
- `boni/`: the Boni dialects' innovations, transcribed from the shaded cells of Table 22 in Elias (2019), with each innovation labelled from the paper's own tables. A test checks the transcription against the figures the paper works out for the Bireeri–Safaree pair.
- `germanic/`: the early Germanic innovations, extracted from the checkmarks of Table 1 in Agee (2018) by their positions on the page. A checkmark with an asterisk (a possible parallel development) or a question mark (a complicated classification) is kept as a 1, and the mark is noted in the innovation's label. A test checks the exclusive counts of Anglo-Frisian, Ingvaeonic and Teuto-Saxon against the thesis's Table 3. The thesis's other figures come from case-by-case tallies in its appendices rather than from the binary table, so they differ somewhat from the scores computed here.

The Sogeram study (Daniels, Barth & Barth 2019) prints only a sample of its 196 innovations, so it cannot be added from the paper alone.

## Code

- `src/client/v2/V2App.tsx` lays out the page and routes to the view; `V2Navigation.tsx` lists the tabs.
- `src/client/v2/glottometry/` holds the data and the pure logic: parsing the tables, scoring the subgroups, placing the languages by coordinates or by likeness, and sampling the padded, rounded hull that a membrane is built from. Each file has a unit test beside it.
- `src/client/v2/scene/` holds the view. `SceneView.tsx` holds the threshold, the appearance and the pointed-at subgroup, and lays out the controls, the scene and the key. `CellScene.tsx` is the Three.js canvas, drawn with React Three Fiber, and `CellBody.tsx` places the membranes, nuclei and axes. `undulation.ts` patches the physical material with the shared displacement field and the edge thickening. `appearance.ts` defines the adjustable settings and their defaults, and `controls/` holds the fields the Appearance panel is made of.
- `e2e/versions.spec.ts` checks that the view shows a canvas.

## Pollination guilds

A first prototype of the glottometric diagram read on biological data: a plant–pollinator network observed at one site, drawn as a cell by the same means as a language family. The point of the analogy is that a glottometric membrane is drawn around languages by an event they underwent together, while here a membrane is drawn around plants by another organism, a visitor, that moves between them. The membranes are made of other organisms, and the diagram can be turned inside out.

The view offers a choice of four small networks, each with its citation and licence shown below the scene. It opens on Île aux Aigrettes:

- Île aux Aigrettes, Mauritius, 1999 (Olesen, Eskildsen & Venkatasamy 2002; Mangal network 934): 13 visitors, 14 plants. The honey bee visits most of the plants, and a day gecko pollinates alongside the insects;
- Flores, Azores, 2000 (same study; Mangal 935): 12 visitors, 10 plants;
- Catimbau, Brazil, 2006 (Bezerra, Machado & Mello 2009; Mangal 929): 13 oil-collecting bees and 13 Malpighiaceae, with visits counted in the thousands;
- Piedmont forest, North Carolina, 1977 to 1979 (Motten 1982; Mangal 945): 44 visitors, 13 spring wildflowers.

- **Bodies** are bright dots, one for each organism of the chosen side of the network, labelled by an abbreviated scientific name. They are placed by likeness, so that bodies sharing many partners lie close together, and bodies that the layout puts in the same place, because they share exactly the same partners, are nudged apart.
- **Guilds** are the membranes: every set of bodies that shares at least one partner on the other side, other than single bodies and the whole site. The whole site is one more membrane around everything.
- The **scores** are those of Historical Glottometry, with a visitor in the place of an innovation: support is the number of partners seen on exactly the guild's members, and cohesiveness the share of partners touching any member whose rounds do not cut across the guild. The key says plainly that a cut means something else here: not evidence against the guild, but an organism that belongs to several guilds at once.
- **Pointing** at a membrane names its guild and the partners that hold it together; pointing at a body names the organism and counts its partners.

### Controls

- The **Site** menu chooses the network.
- **Bodies** chooses which side is drawn as dots, plants or visitors; the other side then forms the membranes.
- The **Encounters seen at least** slider admits only the visits that the survey counted at least that many times; its steps are the network's distinct visit counts. **Replay** sets the minimum to the single most frequent encounter and lowers it step by step, so that the guilds grow and merge as the rarer encounters of the season fill in.
- The **subgroupiness display threshold** slider and the **Appearance** panel are those of the glottometry view. The threshold starts at 0 here, since the guilds of a small network mostly score below 1.

### What the analogy keeps and what it bends

- A shared innovation is an event in the past; a shared visitor is a living partner present at the site, so the membranes here can be flipped to show the visitors grouped by their shared flowers, which a glottometric diagram cannot.
- Innovations are unweighted; visits are counted, so the minimum-visits control is a dimension the linguistic data lacks, and the replay uses it as a stand-in for time.
- A cross-cutting innovation lowers a subgroup's cohesiveness because it argues against the subgroup; a generalist visitor lowers a guild's cohesiveness without arguing against anything. The scores are kept so that the two diagrams can be compared, and the key names the difference.
- Quality flags on the visits (placeholder species, unmatched names) are kept in the data but not yet shown.

### Data

The networks are copied out of the first version's database by `npm run v2:export-pollination` (`scripts/exportPollinationNetworks.ts`) into JSON files under `src/client/v2/pollination/networks/`, one per network, with the visitors, the plants, each visit's count and quality flags, and the study's citation, licence and site. The view bundles those files and reads nothing from the Worker. The script takes the interaction links of each network from `connections` and `connection_sources`, where every link runs from the visitor to the plant.

### Code

- `src/client/v2/pollination/` holds the view and its logic. `GuildView.tsx` holds the chosen network, side, minimum visits, threshold and appearance, and lays out the controls, the scene and the key. `toSharingTable.ts` reads a network as a glottometric table of shared innovations, so that the glottometry folder's scoring and likeness layout apply unchanged. `spreadApart.ts` nudges coincident bodies apart. `GuildScene.tsx` and `GuildBody.tsx` draw the cell with the glottometry scene's membranes, nuclei and materials.
- `scoreSubgroups.ts` now also lists each subgroup's supporting innovations by label, which the guild tooltip shows as the partners holding a guild together.
