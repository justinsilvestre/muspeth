# The second version

This document describes the second version of the app: where it is served, what its one view shows, and where its code lives. The first version is described in [v1.md](v1.md). The two versions share the color scheme setting.

## Addresses

The second version is served under `/v2`, and so far has one view, at `/v2` itself.

## Historical glottometry

The view draws a glottometric diagram as a living cell. Historical Glottometry, a method of Alexandre François and Siva Kalyan, measures every candidate subgroup of a language family by the innovations its languages share, instead of forcing the family into a tree. A glottometric diagram draws each subgroup as a contour around its languages, nested and overlapping where languages belong to several subgroups.

The view shows the sample "Demo" family distributed with the Historical Glottometry engine: eighteen fictitious languages, named ⓁA to ⓁR, with a table of shared innovations and the coordinates of each language.

- **Languages** are bright dots, placed by their coordinates with north upward and east to the right. Two arrows at the southwestern corner show those directions, each spanning half a degree.
- **Subgroups** are translucent lens-shaped membranes enclosing their members. Every set of languages that shares at least one innovation, other than single languages and the whole family, is a candidate subgroup.
- A membrane's **wall is brighter and thicker** the higher the subgroup's subgroupiness.
- Membranes **shade** from one color for the smallest subgroups to another for the largest, and the whole family is one more membrane around everything.
- The whole body **undulates** slowly, so that the diagram reads as a cell with nested organelles rather than a static map.
- **Pointing** at a membrane names its subgroup, with its support, cohesiveness and subgroupiness, below the scene.

### Scores

For a candidate subgroup, following Kalyan and François (2018):

- **Support** is the number of innovations shared by exactly its members.
- **Cohesiveness** is the share of innovations involving any of its members that do not cut across it. An innovation cuts across a subgroup when it is shared by some but not all of the members together with languages outside it.
- **Subgroupiness** is support multiplied by cohesiveness.

### Controls

- The **subgroupiness display threshold** slider hides the subgroups whose subgroupiness is below it, as in the online analyzer. It starts at 1.
- The collapsible **Appearance** panel adjusts the shape of the membranes (depth, margins, corner sharpness), their material (body opacity, edge strength and width, and a glassy, satin or matte finish), their colors, and the movement (amplitude, speed, and whether the camera turns on its own). A Reset button restores the defaults.
- The scene can be turned and zoomed with the pointer.
- The **What you are looking at** panel below the scene explains these elements, and shows the subgroup under the pointer.

## Data

The Demo family's two tables are copied verbatim from the engine's sample data (https://marama.huma-num.fr/AF_Glottometry.htm) into `src/client/v2/glottometry/demo/`, and parsed in the browser. The view reads nothing from the Worker or the database.

## Code

- `src/client/v2/V2App.tsx` lays out the page and routes to the view; `V2Navigation.tsx` lists the tabs.
- `src/client/v2/glottometry/` holds the data and the pure logic: parsing the two tables, scoring the subgroups, placing the languages, and sampling the padded, rounded hull that a membrane is built from. Each file has a unit test beside it.
- `src/client/v2/scene/` holds the view. `SceneView.tsx` holds the threshold, the appearance and the pointed-at subgroup, and lays out the controls, the scene and the key. `CellScene.tsx` is the Three.js canvas, drawn with React Three Fiber, and `CellBody.tsx` places the membranes, nuclei and axes. `undulation.ts` patches the physical material with the shared displacement field and the edge thickening. `appearance.ts` defines the adjustable settings and their defaults, and `controls/` holds the fields the Appearance panel is made of.
- `e2e/versions.spec.ts` checks that the view shows a canvas.
